Essentia dupliciter dicitur.

If you've spent any time evaluating how to make your data stack AI-ready, you've probably encountered ontology and semantic layer used interchangeably, pitched as competitors, or conflated with adjacent concepts like taxonomy, knowledge graph, and business glossary.

The confusion is understandable. Both deal with meaning. Both promise a single source of truth. And both use the word "semantic" liberally.

What is Ontology?

An ontology is a structured, machine-readable representation of domain knowledge that defines classes of things, the properties those things have, and the relationships between them.

What is a semantic layer

A semantic layer is a translation layer between raw data storage and business users that standardizes metrics, dimensions, business terminology, and access rules so every team, tool, and query calculates the same values the same way.

Core Differences

Feature Ontology Semantics / Semantic Layer
Primary Focus Meaning and structure: Models entities, properties, classes, and complex relationships. Measurement and calculation: Standardizes metrics, aggregations, and business logic for reporting.
Core Question "What are the concepts, and how do they connect?" "How do we calculate this metric consistently?"
Primary Consumer Machines, AI agents, and reasoning engines. Humans, business analysts, and BI dashboards.
Origin Field Artificial intelligence and knowledge management. Business Intelligence (BI) and data warehousing.
Example Defining that a Customer has a location, belongs to a risk tier, and links to an Account. Defining that Quarterly Revenue equals the sum of completed orders minus tax.
