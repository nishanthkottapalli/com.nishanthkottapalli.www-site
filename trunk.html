Work conquers all.

Labor omnia vincit

Trunks are the stable bodies of knowledge that connect broad source domains to specialised capabilities and the artefacts they produce. Tools, projects and ventures may change; a mature trunk accumulates concepts, methods, evidence and practice over time.

The canonical model 

Ground — reasoning, observation, questions, claims, evidence and decisions
└── Roots - broad source domains and enduring interests
    └── Trunks - coherent bodies of knowledge
        └── Branches - specialised topics, practices and capabilities
            └── Leaves - projects, repositories, writing, experiments and other artefacts

A trunk is not a personal principle, a working method, an organisation, a product or an individual project. Principles belong in Ground; organisations and ventures sit alongside the tree; products and projects normally appear as Leaves.

How trunks behave

Stable, not static

A trunk persists while its terminology, tools and branches evolve. Computer Science remains a body of knowledge even as languages, frameworks and runtime platforms change.

Connected to several roots

A trunk may draw from more than one domain. Game Design grows from Arts & Entertainment, Technology & Computing, Hobbies & Interests and Business.

Expressed through branches

Trunks become actionable through narrower subjects, practices and capabilities such as Detection Engineering, Game AI, Platform Engineering or Marketing Analytics.

Proven through leaves

Repositories, games, assessments, experiments, articles, diagrams and case studies provide inspectable evidence that a body of knowledge is actually being developed and applied.

Principles that support every trunk

Learning by building

Real understanding grows through systems that have to work and expose design trade-offs, failure modes and new questions.

From fundamentals to systems

Foundational concepts should remain reusable while their applications connect games, infrastructure, AI, products and physical systems.

Public knowledge assets

Useful outcomes should leave behind durable evidence: repositories, writing, diagrams, demos, templates, devlogs, reports or case studies.

Compounding evolution

The aim is not to collect unrelated projects. Each branch and leaf should deepen one or more trunks and strengthen the connected body of work.

These are Ground-level principles that guide how the trunks are developed. They are not themselves trunks.
